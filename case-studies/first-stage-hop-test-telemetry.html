Telemetry downlink for first-stage hop tests at 130 km

Excerpt from a reference specification for the telemetry downlink of take-off and landing tests of a reusable first stage. The flight cannot be repeated, so the specification makes one promise: the ground archive is complete even when the radio link is not. It is a public reference: frequencies, rates and margins are illustrative and do not describe a specific vehicle, range or client. Guidance, landing control, the command link and flight termination are separate systems and are not part of this document.

1Scope

This document specifies the telemetry downlink from the flight segment to the ground segment during hop tests, the onboard recording, and the ground processing that produces the test archive. It does not cover guidance, landing control, the command uplink or flight termination.

3Requirements

  1. TLM-SYS-001

    Every telemetry frame generated on board between power-on and post-flight shutdown shall be present in the ground archive after post-flight processing.

    T

    The flight cannot be repeated.

  2. TLM-SYS-002

    Class A data shall be available in real time with frame loss below 10⁻⁴ in every flight phase.

    AT

    The test team decides on live health data.

  3. TLM-RF-001

    The link shall provide at least 20 dB margin at 130 km at the worst usable onboard antenna aspect.

    A

    Budget for plume, nulls and multipath.

  4. TLM-GND-001

    A second ground station shall cover the landing zone below the radio horizon of the launch-site station.

    A

    Geometry, see §4.2.

  5. TLM-FRM-001

    Each transfer frame shall carry a frame counter and onboard time, and shall be protected by a CRC.

    I

    Gap detection and ordering on the ground.

  6. TLM-FRM-002

    Coding shall correct a burst of 5 ms at the nominal data rate without loss.

    AT

    Antenna nulls and multipath bursts.

  7. TLM-REC-001

    Every frame shall be written to the recorder before transmission.

    IT

    The recorder is the reference copy.

  8. TLM-REC-002

    After loss of signal, missed class B frames shall be re-sent oldest first when the link recovers.

    T

    Engineering data available before landing.

4.2Radio horizon

d ≈ 4.12 · (√h_A + √h_V) [km, heights in m, 4/3 Earth] d = 130 km, h_A = 10 m → √h_V ≥ 130 / 4.12 − √10 ≈ 28.4 → h_V ≥ ≈ 806 m

Below about 800 m the launch-site station cannot see the stage at 130 km at any transmitter power. TLM-GND-001 follows from geometry.

The frame structure follows CCSDS TM Space Data Link conventions; field sizes in bytes.

FieldSizeContent
Attached sync marker40x1ACFFC1D

Data fieldvariablePackets of the virtual channel
Frame error control2CRC-16 over the frame
VCClassContentLive handling
0AHealth, events, phase markersAlways sent; each packet sent twice, Δt apart
1BEngineering measurementsSent while margin allows; backfilled after outage
2CHigh-rate dataBest effort live; complete from recorder
7—Idle fillKeeps the receiver locked

Coding layer: inner convolutional code and outer Reed–Solomon RS(255, 223), t = 16 bytes per codeword, block interleaving to depth I = 80 (deeper than the standard CCSDS depths, sized for the burst requirement TLM-FRM-002).

6Protection against data loss

Five layers, each for a different kind of loss.

LayerHandlesMechanism
1 FECRandom bit errorsConcatenated code; no retransmission needed
2 InterleavingBursts up to 5 msRS(255, 223), depth 80
3 Station diversityPlume fades, horizonBest copy per frame counter from A or B
4 Classes, time diversityFades during live monitoringClass A sent twice, Δt apart
5 Recorder and gap fillEverything elseBackfill in flight, full dump after landing
burst at 2 Mbit/s, 5 ms: 2·10⁶ · 0.005 / 8 = 1250 bytes correctable if burst ≤ t · I → I ≥ 1250 / 16 ≈ 78 → I = 80 interleaver latency: 80 · 255 bytes / 250 000 B/s ≈ 82 ms two stations, independent: P_loss = 10⁻² · 10⁻² = 10⁻⁴ backfill after 20 s outage: T = 20 s · 0.4 Mbit/s / 0.6 Mbit/s ≈ 13 s recorder for 10 min at 2 Mbit/s: 1.2 Gbit ≈ 150 MB

Independence of the two stations is designed, not assumed: the plume attenuates along the stage axis, so A and B are placed to see the stage from different aspect angles.
