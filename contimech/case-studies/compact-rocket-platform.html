Compact rocket platform: models before metal

A compact rocket platform had to be defined as an engineering system before detailed design: geometry, motor boundary, payload mass, steering surfaces, actuation and power had to fit together. We answered the open questions with a written baseline, models and a quick physical prototype first.

Platform
40 cm body, ready motor
Baseline
payload ≈ 110 g, burn ≈ 20 s
Tools
FreeCAD, MATLAB, Simulink
Services
1.1 Prototyping · 1.2 Analysis · 1.3 Modelling

Task

The job was not to sketch a shape. Airframe, propulsion boundary, steering concept, payload mass, models and implementation assumptions had to agree before deeper mechanical and modelling work started, so that later steps would not be built on guesses.

Baseline

ItemValueTreated as
Body length≈ 40 cmGeometric envelope
Payload mass≈ 110 gAllocated system boundary for sizing and packaging
MotorReady solid motor, burn ≈ 20 sFixed interface; powered segment modelled explicitly
SteeringThree rear aerodynamic surfacesSelected concept, sized against actuation and packaging

What we did

  • System split. Body, propulsion, surfaces, actuation, power and models separated into work packages with visible dependencies.
  • Models first. Motor-burn and flight-behaviour models in MATLAB/Simulink to reason about trajectory, altitude and range, and the effect of each design assumption.
  • Sizing. Actuator selection and battery capacity derived from the integration case, not picked from a catalogue.
  • Layout. FreeCAD body and rear packaging around the real motor boundary and the three surfaces.
  • Prototype check. A quick physical build to confirm proportions and the practical feel of the concept in parallel with digital design.

Result

A compact, reviewable baseline: size and mass constraints, propulsion assumption, steering concept and model-supported reasoning aligned before detailed implementation. The next mechanical and modelling steps started without open questions about the basics.
